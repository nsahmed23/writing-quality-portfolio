# Garner’s Language-Change Index

| Stage | Garner’s first-party label | Portfolio interpretation |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Rejected | Small-minority innovation/dialectal form treated as an error in standard edited usage |
| 2 | Widely shunned | Spread significantly but remains unacceptable in standard usage |
| 3 | Widespread but… | Common, including among educated users, but generally avoided in careful edited usage |
| 4 | Ubiquitous but… | Nearly universal with residual objection from some usage authorities |
| 5 | Fully accepted | Accepted in standard usage apart from fringe objection |

Source: Garner’s [first-party LCI explanation](https://lawprose.org/garners-usage-tip-of-the-day-language-change-index-5/). The scale embeds prescriptive judgments about “careful” and “educated” use. It is not a frequency threshold, grammaticality theorem, or guarantee that a 2022 ranking remains current.

First-party examples used only to demonstrate the scale: *illustrative* for *illustrious* (1), *staunch the flow* for *stanch* (2), *not as good of a* (3), object *who* outside a following preposition (4), and plural agreement with *none* (5). Always retrieve an issue’s actual fifth-edition entry before asserting its stage.
