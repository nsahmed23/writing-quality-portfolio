# Concision

Reduce reader work per unit of meaning, not word count at any cost. Combine Williams–Bizup’s redundancy diagnostics with Lanham’s paramedic procedure, then repair rhythm and syntax after cutting. [WB-CON-01–08; LAN-PM-01–10]

## Establish the preservation floor

Before cutting, list the proposition, scope, agent, conditions, exceptions, degree of certainty, technical terms, citations, politeness requirements, and legal/scientific consequences. Treat each as protected unless the user authorizes a substantive change.

## Diagnose genuine excess

Flag only language whose removal or consolidation preserves the protected meaning:

- empty metadiscourse or throat-clearing that does not orient the reader;
- redundant pairs, modifiers already entailed by a head, or repeated propositions;
- long prepositional chains and empty introductions that delay the clause;
- abstract action-noun plus light-verb constructions when the main cost is excess;
- hedges stacked beyond the evidentiary need, intensifiers without calibrated force, or self-evident attribution;
- official-style impersonality, inflated diction, and jargon that adds status rather than precision. [WB-CON-02–07; LAN-OFF-01–04]

Do not count necessary definitions, navigation, courtesy, conditions, evidence, transitions, or deliberate repetition as excess.

## Apply a guarded paramedic pass

1. Mark long prepositional phrases; determine which express required relations.
2. Circle forms of *be* and other light verbs; decide whether they express identity/state or merely carry a buried action.
3. Locate the central action and responsible actor.
4. Cut empty openings and duplicative framing.
5. Turn a buried action into a verb when doing so shortens the sentence without losing a useful concept or topic.
6. Prefer a direct subject–verb core when it preserves information flow and agency.
7. Rebuild the sentence after deletion; do not leave telegram prose, broken coordination, or ambiguity.
8. Read for rhythm, courtesy, register, and precision.
9. Compute the lard factor only as an audit statistic: `(original words - revised words) / original words`. Never use a target percentage as a quality threshold. [LAN-PM-01–10]

## Resolve collisions

- `sentence-clarity` owns actor/action alignment when length is not the primary problem.
- `cohesion-emphasis` can require a passive, nominalization, transition, or repeated topic that this pass must retain.
- `sentence-variety` runs after cutting, because compression changes clause architecture and rhythm.
- Preserve the longer version when it is more accurate, appropriately courteous, or easier to parse.

## Stop conditions

Stop when every remaining word contributes propositional content, logic, navigation, tone, precision, qualification, or rhythm; further deletion would change meaning/register or create repair work; and the revision is no harder to read than the original. Leave already concise prose unchanged.

## Return proportionate output

- **Targeted review:** `keep` or `cut`, exact excess category, one revision, protected information check.
- **Direct revision:** concise text first; report meaningful deletions or compression percentage only if useful.
- **Fault explanation:** identify duplicated or empty content, not merely a disliked word.
- **Whole-document audit:** repeated patterns, representative edits, high-risk cuts, measured savings.
- **Broad polish:** operate after structure/flow/clarity; do not redo their work.
