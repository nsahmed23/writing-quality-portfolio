# Sentence Variety

Use syntax to organize attention and relationships. Variety is a means, never the objective; retain repetition when it creates clarity, cadence, comparison, or emphasis. [TUF-ORG-01; TUF-SYM-01]

## Diagnose architecture, not length alone

1. Identify the passage’s rhetorical jobs: sequence, accumulation, contrast, qualification, definition, cause, suspense, emphasis, or closure.
2. Map several consecutive sentence openings, main-clause patterns, branch directions, coordination/subordination, and closure shapes.
3. Flag repetition only when form and rhetorical job are misaligned, or when identical architecture makes relationships hard to perceive.
4. Separately flag choppiness (related propositions stranded in equal short units) and sprawl (uncontrolled or ambiguously attached units).
5. Stop when repeated forms are deliberate and effective. [TUF-SHORT-01; TUF-BR-01–04]

## Select form by function

- **Short independent sentence:** contrast, decision, warning, or closure.
- **Coordination / parallelism:** equal weight, comparison, sequence, or accumulation.
- **Subordinate adverbial clause:** cause, condition, concession, time, or hierarchy.
- **Relative or appositive unit:** identify or add compact supplementary information.
- **Participial, infinitival, or prepositional phrase:** compress a clearly controlled secondary relation; require an unambiguous attachment.
- **Cumulative/right-branching sentence:** state a core, then add specifications or consequences.
- **Periodic/left-branching sentence:** hold context or conditions before a consequential main clause; use only when the delay is manageable.
- **Mid-branch/interruption:** insert a brief qualification exactly where needed; avoid severing long dependencies.
- **Absolute construction:** frame a circumstance with an explicit noun–predicate relation.
- **Inversion or delayed subject/predicate:** redirect attention or control revelation; reserve for a real emphasis need.
- **Question, imperative, exclamation, or fragment:** direct interaction, instruction, marked emphasis, or deliberate voice; respect register.
- **Antithesis, balance, repetition, ellipsis, asyndeton, polysyndeton:** make a logical/rhythmic relation perceptible; avoid imitation and display.
- **Parentheses/dashes/colons/semicolons:** control hierarchy and boundary, not cosmetic variation. [TUF-CAT-01–22]

## Revise safely

1. Choose one relationship the current syntax obscures.
2. Select the least marked form that expresses it.
3. Preserve propositions, emphasis, references, technical terms, citations, voice, and register.
4. Check modifier attachment, coordination scope, tense, agreement, punctuation, and breath/readability.
5. Compare in context. Revert if the new form adds decoration, ambiguity, or false hierarchy.

## Coordinate with adjacent skills

Run after structure, cohesion, clarity, and concision: those passes can invalidate rhythm decisions. Never add words deleted for genuine excess merely to create a longer pattern. Do not unwind technical nominalizations or passives solely for variety.

## Return proportionate output

- **Targeted review:** architecture diagnosis, rhetorical purpose, chosen form, one revision.
- **Direct revision:** revised text plus a one-line form/function rationale when non-obvious.
- **Fault explanation:** show the repeated structure, not just sentence lengths.
- **Whole-document audit:** opener/clause-pattern inventory, consequential monotony, choppiness/sprawl, selective repairs.
- **Broad polish:** intervene only after earlier passes stabilize; leave effective patterns alone.
