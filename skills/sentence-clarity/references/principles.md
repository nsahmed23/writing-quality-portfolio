# Sentence-clarity principles

| ID | Observable signal | Diagnostic question | Operation | Stop/exception |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WB-CLA-01 | Reader cannot readily state who/what does what | What proposition does the clause encode? | Recover actor/action/state before changing words | Do not force an eventive story onto identity or state clauses |
| WB-CLA-02 | Key action appears in an action noun beside a light verb | Does verbing the noun expose the main action? | Promote the action to a finite verb | Keep terms of art, results, backward links, and useful concepts |
| WB-CLA-03 | Shell/abstract subject hides the responsible entity | Does agency matter to interpretation or accountability? | Use the supported actor as subject | Abstract/nonhuman topics can be exact characters |
| WB-CLA-04 | Long windup delays the clause kernel | Does the opener provide needed context before the claim? | Move, compress, or split the opener | Retain manageable periodic syntax with a purpose |
| WB-CLA-05 | Passive suppresses or backgrounds an agent | Does suppression harm accountability or comprehension? | Recover agent only when known and relevant | Keep passive for topic continuity, unknown/irrelevant agent, or affected-entity focus |
| WB-CLA-06 | Subject and finite verb are separated by a heavy movable unit | Does the reader lose the dependency or mis-rank the interruption? | Move/shorten/promote the unit | Short or essential modifiers may intervene |
| WB-CLA-07 | Revision is merely more active/concrete | Is there a reader problem after context is considered? | Keep original | Minimal intervention controls |
| WB-ETH-01 | Edit changes scope, certainty, agency, attribution, or evidence | Can every protected proposition be aligned? | Restore changed meaning or reject edit | No stylistic gain overrides semantic fidelity |

Use morphology and forms of *be/have/do/make* only as search clues. A valid fault requires the reader problem in the table.

Old/given-before-new and stress placement belong to `cohesion-emphasis`; redundancy belongs to `concision`.
