# Reader-expectation model

Complete operational extraction of Gopen–Swan’s article:

1. Structural locations cue the role of material.
2. Follow a grammatical subject reasonably soon with its verb when an interruption would be mis-ranked or burdensome.
3. Syntactic closure creates a stress candidate; colon/semicolon can create subsidiary closure.
4. One discourse unit should offer a recoverable principal stress, though long sentences can work.
5. Topic position supplies backward linkage and a perspective for reading forward.
6. Old/accessible information often creates the backward link; new/emphasis-worthy material often belongs later.
7. Do not convert this into “all old first, all new last.” Linkage, context, and intended emphasis decide.
8. Passive can better preserve an established topic or background an irrelevant agent.
9. Put interpretive context before dependent technical details.
10. Let the main-clause verb carry the principal action when that action is the point.
11. Use clause hierarchy to signal primary and secondary material.
12. Add a missing logical premise when flow revision exposes one; empty transitions cannot substitute.
13. Related topic-position material forms a topic string.
14. Paragraph progression should make movement recoverable.
15. Prefer syntactic emphasis before typography.

Evidence status: case-supported rhetorical heuristics. The 1990 article reports no controlled participants, comprehension measures, or statistical experiment. Independent information-structure and dependency-length research supports components, not the complete fixed-position model.
