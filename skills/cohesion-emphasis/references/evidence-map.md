# Evidence map

Primary full text: George D. Gopen and Judith A. Swan, “The Science of Scientific Writing,” *American Scientist* 78.6 (1990), 550–558; [Duke record](https://scholars.duke.edu/display/pub881487), [full permission-reprinted article](https://people.tamu.edu/~khuffenberger/resources/science-of-writing.pdf). IDs GS-RE-01–10 and the reader-model inventory derive from the full article.

Gopen’s *The Sense of Structure* (Pearson Longman, 2004/©2005, ISBN 9780205296323) is TOC/subheading-accounted but was not fully accessible. It has six chapters covering sentence complexity/anatomy, weights/connections, paragraph shape, bad advice, and punctuation; no book page-level extraction is claimed.

Williams/Bizup IDs map to Lessons 4 “Cohesion and Coherence,” 5 “Emphasis,” 6 “Global Coherence,” and 8 “Shape” in the [official fifth-edition TOC](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/style-the-basics-of-clarity-and-grace/P200000002141/9780134109749); full lessons unavailable.

Independent component evidence: Junge, Theakston & Lieven on given/new ordering ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716413000350)); Temperley on probabilistic dependency-length minimization ([PDF](https://davidtemperley.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/temperley-cog07.pdf)); Futrell et al. on large-scale dependency length ([article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4547262/)). These do not validate universal “fixed” reader positions.
