# Cohesion and Emphasis

Guide readers from accessible context to consequential new information while aligning syntactic prominence with intended importance. Treat Gopen’s reader-expectation model as a strong editorial heuristic, not a universal cognitive law. [WB-COH-01–08; WB-EMP-01–06; GS-RE-01–10]

## Map before editing

1. State the paragraph’s point and intended movement.
2. For each sentence, record the grammatical topic/subject, backward link, new information, main-clause action, and final stress candidate.
3. Trace the **topic string**: repeated, pronominal, superordinate, or logically linked topics. Mark only shifts the reader cannot infer.
4. Trace references and lexical chains. Identify ambiguous pronouns, missing antecedents, and empty transitions that name no relation.
5. Check whether each sentence begins with information sufficiently accessible from prior discourse and ends with material that advances the passage.
6. Check whether syntactic hierarchy makes the intended main claim the main clause and puts qualifications where their scope is clear.
7. Check subject–verb distance and end weight; flag them only when the dependency or intended emphasis becomes hard to recover. [GS-RE-02–08]

## Repair in dependency order

1. Fix paragraph purpose and sentence order before local wording.
2. Restore a backward link in the opening topic position through repetition, pronoun, demonstrative summary, or a logically superordinate term.
3. Move context/given material before dependent new material when the reader otherwise lacks interpretive context.
4. Put the intended payoff near a syntactic closure or in a short final sentence; do not assume the final word always receives emphasis.
5. Put the central action in the main clause and qualifications in positions that preserve their true scope.
6. Shorten a harmful subject–verb gap or recast the clause without flattening useful complexity.
7. Use passive voice when the affected entity is the established topic or the agent would break continuity; recover the agent when suppression impairs accountability.
8. Remove a transition if it only labels a relation that syntax or order still fails to express.
9. Reread the paragraph for continuity and emphasis, then audit meaning. [WB-COH-02–08; GS-RE-01–10]

## Distinguish terms

- **Topic position:** opening structural position used to anchor a sentence; not automatically the grammatical subject.
- **Given/accessible information:** recoverable from discourse or shared context; not synonymous with “already used words.”
- **Sentence topic:** entity/proposition the sentence is construed as being about.
- **Stress position:** closure region that often receives default emphasis; contrastive syntax and punctuation can create other stress positions.
- **End weight:** tendency to place longer/complex constituents later; separate from informational newness.

## Exceptions and evidence limits

Given-before-new is a preference with competing motivations, not a ban on new-first openings. Contrast, surprise, chronological iconicity, genre conventions, or a deliberate topic shift can control. Gopen–Swan explicitly present their principles as revisable heuristics, and independent research supports information-structure and dependency-length effects without validating every “fixed expectation” claim.

## Preserve and stop

Preserve logical order, citations, topic terms, scope of qualifiers, warranted hedges, agents, voice, and technical dependencies. Stop when every sentence has an intelligible backward relation or intentional break; topics progress; hierarchy matches importance; and further transitions/repetition would be redundant.

## Return proportionate output

- **Targeted review:** flow/emphasis diagnosis, map of the relevant link, one revision.
- **Direct revision:** revised passage first; note passive/topic/stress choices when consequential.
- **Fault explanation:** identify missing link, misordered information, false hierarchy, or misplaced stress.
- **Whole-document audit:** paragraph maps, topic-string breaks, hierarchy/stress patterns, prioritized repairs.
- **Broad polish:** run after document/argument structure and before sentence clarity/concision.
