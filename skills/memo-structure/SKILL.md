# Memo Structure

Build a traceable hierarchy of thought before polishing sentences. Treat pyramid structure and MECE as reasoning tests, not formatting ornaments. [MIN-WR-01–02; MIN-PYR-01–05; MIN-PS-01–06]

## Decide whether this genre fits

Use this skill when a decision-maker needs an answer, recommendation, conclusion, or defensible synthesis. Skip or limit it for discovery writing, chronological incident records, stories, tutorials, direct reference, or research whose conclusion is not yet warranted. An explicit user request controls unless it would misrepresent evidence.

## Recover the governing logic

1. Name the audience, decision, deadline, and constraints.
2. Formulate the single governing question the document must answer.
3. State the best-supported answer in one complete sentence, including uncertainty or conditions. Never manufacture confidence for a clean pyramid.
4. List the minimum key-line supports that together justify the answer.
5. For each support, ask the vertical question it raises (*why? how? what?*) and verify that the children answer that question.
6. Test each sibling group horizontally: same logical kind, no unexplained overlap, no material gap, and one explicit ordering principle.
7. Summarize each group with a claim, not a category label or topic noun. [MIN-PYR-01–05]

## Choose horizontal logic

- Use **deduction** when the argument depends on a rule/premise, a case, and an implication. Keep the chain short and expose contestable premises.
- Use **induction** when comparable observations support a generalization. Group like evidence and state the inference conservatively.
- Order by time for processes, by structure for parts of a whole, or by degree/rank for priorities. Do not mix orders silently. [MIN-ORD-01–04]
- Use MECE as a practical coverage check. Natural systems can overlap; if boundaries are analytically useful but imperfect, name the overlap instead of forcing false exclusivity. [MIN-MECE-01–03]

## Build the opening

Use SCQA only when it helps the reader recognize the governing question:

1. **Situation:** shared, relevant context.
2. **Complication:** the change, tension, or obstacle.
3. **Question:** the decision/problem the complication raises, stated or clearly implied.
4. **Answer:** the governing conclusion.

For urgent decisions, lead with the answer and compress context. For sensitive, adversarial, unfamiliar, or evidentially incomplete matters, enough context may precede the answer to make it interpretable and fair. Do not force every email or report into all four labels. [MIN-SCQA-01–05]

## Build top-down or bottom-up

- **Top-down:** start from a known question and provisional answer; test and replace unsupported branches.
- **Bottom-up:** sort observations into genuine relationships, infer summaries, then test whether the emerging answer addresses the audience’s question.
- Separate problem definition, diagnosis, option generation, evaluation, and recommendation. Do not let a presentation framework substitute for analysis. [MIN-PS-01–06]

## Preserve evidence

Keep contrary evidence, assumptions, unknowns, confidence, owners, dates, citations, and dependencies visible. A neat grouping never licenses deletion of inconvenient facts. Distinguish document logic from visual indentation and slide layout.

## Stop conditions

Stop when the answer addresses the governing question; every parent summarizes its children; sibling groups use one defensible relation and order; material gaps/overlaps are resolved or disclosed; and the opening prepares the intended reader without genre distortion.

## Return proportionate output

- **Targeted review:** governing question, answer, and the failing vertical/horizontal link.
- **Direct revision:** answer-first draft plus a compact hierarchy when useful.
- **Fault explanation:** show the broken parent-child or sibling relationship and its consequence.
- **Whole-document audit:** audience/question/answer, outline tree, overlap-gap table, opening test, repair sequence.
- **Broad polish:** change structure first; defer flow and sentence passes until the hierarchy stabilizes.
