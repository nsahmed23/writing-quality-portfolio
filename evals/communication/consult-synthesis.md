## Where they split

1. **Is "lead with the next action" universal or conditional on the request?** GPT (Q5, Q6): replace the universal rule with "lead with what the user requested: the answer, deliverable, verified result, or next executable action; do not manufacture an action for an explanatory or artifact-only request." B, Kimi, Claude: keep action-first as the default and carve out explain and editing as overrides. This is the only split that changes the skill's identity. The iteration data lean toward GPT: the JWT explain eval is flat either way, the true-premise regression came from forcing a shape onto a why-question, and the editing boundary needs the same carve-out anyway. The cost is a softer rule that a weaker model may under-apply.
2. **Time estimates.** GPT: only when useful and grounded, with assumptions and a range; the 25-line candidate drops the mandate. Kimi, Claude, B: keep, one clause after step 1. Reconciled text: "When the reader will execute something and the estimate has a basis, put it in one clause after the first step; never invent one to satisfy a rule."
3. **JWT explain prompt.** GPT: keep as regression coverage. B, Kimi: drop as non-discriminating. Claude: demote to a rule-1 over-application check. Reconciled: keep, scored as an over-application check (does the skill force a fake action line on an explanation?), excluded from the headline.
4. **Re-explanation of a wrong previous answer.** GPT alone raises it: "facts survive verbatim" becomes "errors survive verbatim". Add GPT's `git clean -fd` fixture and a correctness exception to the re-explain rule. No reviewer disagrees; the others did not consider it.
5. **Which short candidate to test.** Four texts exist: Claude's 22 lines and B's and Kimi's 25 lines keep the shape rules; GPT's 25 lines are policy-level and drop universal action framing, mandatory estimates, the list cap, and one-line steps. Testing GPT's candidate against the full skill is the informative comparison because it embodies split 1; the shape-preserving lists are compressions of the full skill and would mostly measure the value of the examples.
6. **Sequence.** B: v4 edit now, then three arms on Opus. GPT and Claude: three arms on the target model with v4 frozen first. Kimi: a multi-turn simulated-user suite first. B also proposes the cheapest step of all: blind pairwise on the existing iteration-3 corpus with an off-family judge, zero new executor runs.
7. **Deployment.** B: put the short version in CLAUDE.md now (it is the only always-on mechanism; output styles do not reach subagents; `/output-style` was removed in v2.1.91, selection is under `/config`). GPT: CLAUDE.md is the always-on mechanism, but measure loading and context cost in the real deployment before choosing. Claude: decide after the three-arm run.

## Recommendation

v4 skill edits, all from the consensus rows: caveat placement (B Q5 text), multi-step closing line as the check that shows the steps worked (B), brevity as a closed deletion list with the checklist (B Q7 plus GPT Q7's merge condition), vocabulary rewrite with conditions and the positive case (B's post-iteration-3 text, GPT Q8's paired examples), scope-aware destructive override with B's two guards and GPT's "merged into the intended target" check, GPT Q11's seam paragraph plus Claude's pre-send scope sentence, and the estimate reconciliation above. Then the one design choice: adopt GPT's conditional rule 1 (split 1), keeping "for procedural help, the first line is the first action" explicit so the crisp case stays crisp.

Evals: adopt GPT Q1's aggregation corrections (applicability from the task; report per prompt; blocked execution kept distinct), add the preservation inventory and reader-execution test, add the artifact-only fixture, the wrong-previous-answer re-explain fixture, the multi-issue prompt, the expert-reader prompt, and the second destructive fixture; drop character caps; move the six opener/closer checks and the JWT prompt out of the headline.

Sequence: (1) blind pairwise on the iteration-3 corpus now with an off-family judge (24 pairs, both orders, GPT Q4's four outcomes and B's question wording), zero executor cost; (2) v4 edits; (3) three arms on the target model per GPT Q12 with GPT's 25-line candidate as arm C; (4) Kimi's multi-turn suite as iteration 5 if the three-arm result keeps the skill alive. Deployment waits for (3), because (3) is exactly the question of which text to deploy.

## Decisions for the user

1. Split 1: conditional action framing (recommended) or keep universal action-first.
2. Off-family judge for the pairwise pass: Kimi via the local CLI (uses membership quota) or GPT-5.6 via Codex.
3. Deployment now (B's argument) or after the three-arm run (recommended).
