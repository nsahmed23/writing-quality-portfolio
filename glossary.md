# Canonical glossary

| Term | Definition | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Actor / character | Entity central to the clause’s represented story; may be person, organization, system, object, or concept | Not necessarily agent, subject, concrete noun, or animate being |
| Agent | Semantic participant responsible for an action | May be absent, unknown, irrelevant, or not the grammatical subject |
| Grammatical subject | Syntactic relation conventionally controlling agreement/inversion | Not automatically actor, agent, topic, or given information |
| Sentence/discourse topic | Entity or proposition the unit is construed as being about | May be expressed outside the grammatical subject |
| Topic position | Opening structural region used to anchor backward connection/perspective in Gopen’s model | A position, not a synonym for subject or given information |
| Given / accessible information | Material recoverable from prior discourse, shared knowledge, or immediate situation | “Old” is functional accessibility, not merely repeated wording |
| New information | Material not yet integrated into the discourse model | Not necessarily most important or longest |
| Context position | Opening material that supplies an interpretive frame | One function of an opening; not identical to topic position |
| Stress position | Closure region of a syntactic/rhetorical unit likely to receive default emphasis | Not always the literal final word or spoken phonological stress |
| End weight | Probabilistic tendency to place structurally heavy constituents later | Separate from newness and emphasis |
| Nominalization | Noun morphologically/semantically related to a verb/adjective | A form, not a fault; diagnose only a functionally buried action |
| Light verb | Semantically general verb such as *make*, *perform*, or *be* in a construction | Can carry real state/aspect/collocational meaning |
| Passive | Clause whose subject corresponds to a non-agentive participant and whose verb has passive syntax | Not synonymous with vague, indirect, past tense, or any *be* clause |
| Cohesion | Recoverable local ties among sentences: reference, lexical chains, accessible openings | Distinct from logical coherence |
| Coherence | Reader’s ability to recover a unit’s point, relations, and progression | Cannot be supplied by transitions alone |
| SCQ / SCQA | Situation–Complication–Question, plus the governing Answer in common portfolio shorthand | Minto’s current site brands SCQ Framework; do not mislabel SCQA as her only/current term |
| MECE | Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive grouping test | Practical analytic aim, not a truth guarantee or ban on natural overlap |
| Tutorial | Managed learning through meaningful action in an acquisition context | Not simply easy, step-based, or for “beginners” |
| How-to guide | Goal-oriented directions for a competent user at work | Not a teaching journey or product tour |
| Reference | Neutral, accurate, consistent, scope-complete description for lookup | Examples illustrate; they do not turn it into a tutorial |
| Explanation | Contextual understanding of causes, relations, tradeoffs, and alternatives | Not procedure or exhaustive specification |
| Language-Change Index | Garner’s five-stage prescriptive acceptance scale | Not grammaticality, frequency alone, or timeless status |
| Skunked term | Form/sense whose dispute may distract enough to justify recasting | Avoidance is pragmatic, not proof of inherent error |
| Lard factor | Lanham’s descriptive percentage of words removed | Not a target or quality score |
| Syntactic variety | Purposeful selection among grammatical architectures | Not random length variation |
